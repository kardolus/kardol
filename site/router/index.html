The home router

A small OPNsense box that every packet in the house goes through. It strips out ads and trackers for every device at once, encrypts every DNS lookup, lets me choose which country each device appears to be in, and brings me home from anywhere.

It also sits in front of forge, the basement Kubernetes cluster, and is how I reach it when I'm out.

Ad blocking, for every device at once

No browser extensions, no per-device apps. The TV, the phones, the guests' laptops: everything that joins the network gets the same filtering, because everything asks the router for directions.

A DNS lookup's journey

what happens when anything in the house looks up a name
A device asks"Where is ads.example.com?" DHCP told every device to ask the router.
→
AdGuard checks~410k rules for ads, trackers and malware. A match gets a dead end, and the ad never loads.
→
Unbound resolvesEverything else goes to the local resolver, which caches answers and checks DNSSEC signatures.
→
Encrypted upstreamOnly cache misses leave the house, over DNS-over-TLS. The internet provider sees none of it.

On a normal evening about one lookup in eleven is something the house didn't want: an ad, a tracker, telemetry.

No side doors

Some devices try to skip local DNS with their own encrypted resolver. The firewall blocks DNS-over-TLS out of the house, so they fall back to the router and get filtered like everything else.

Safe Browsing on

Known phishing and malware domains are blocked for everyone, on top of the ad and tracker lists.

A country per device

Any device in the house can be sent out through a VPN exit in another country, while everything else uses the normal connection. No VPN app on the device; the device doesn't even know.

Yes, the Apple TV really does stream from Berlin.

Home, from anywhere

The router runs a WireGuard server for the family's phones and laptops. Away from home, they connect back and behave as if they never left.

Away from home

a phone on hotel Wi-Fi or a cell network
Phone connectsWireGuard tunnels home over whatever network it's on. Nothing else on the router is reachable from outside.
→
Home DNSLookups go through the tunnel to AdGuard, so ads are blocked on the road too.
→
No DNS leaksThe hotel network never sees a lookup: they're inside the tunnel, then encrypted again on the way out.
→
The LAN is right therePhotos back up to forge directly, which skips Cloudflare's size limits for big videos.

Tailscale too

The router is also a Tailscale node that routes to the home network. That's how I reach forge and the router's own admin from a laptop anywhere.

Nothing else open

The websites in the house reach the internet through Cloudflare tunnels that dial out. The router forwards no ports at all. The only thing listening is WireGuard, which ignores anyone without a key.

The quiet plumbing

DHCP with reservations

Kea hands out addresses and pins forge's, after a power cut once let the printer grab it and took the whole cluster down.

Backed up nightly

forge pulls the router's configuration (with its full revision history) every night into the same encrypted backups that go offsite.

Learned the hard way

A hand-installed service once ran in the foreground at boot, so the router never finished starting: no DHCP, no cron. It now runs supervised in the background, and a test reboot proved it.
